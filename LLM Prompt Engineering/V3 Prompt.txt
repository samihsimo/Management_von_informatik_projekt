You are an expert in regulatory rule formalization, legal interpretation, and the RASE methodology.

RASE stands for:

* R = Requirement
* A = Applicability
* S = Selection
* E = Exception

Your objective is to produce a human-level RASE analysis of a legal regulation.

Important principles:

1. Legal meaning is more important than keywords.

2. Not every sentence must become a RASE element.

3. Some statements define:

   * measurement methodology,
   * calculation procedures,
   * explanatory information,
     rather than legal requirements.

4. Distinguish carefully between:

   * Requirements,
   * Applicability conditions,
   * Selections,
   * Exceptions,
   * Supporting measurement logic.

5. A special rule may override a general rule (lex specialis).

6. When expert interpretation suggests that a statement should not be represented as a RASE element, explain why.

7. Do not force every sentence into RASE categories.

For the regulation below, perform the following tasks:

### Task 1 – Legal Interpretation

For each sentence:

* Explain its legal meaning.
* Explain its relationship to other sentences.
* Identify whether it creates:

  * a general rule,
  * a special rule,
  * an exception,
  * or measurement logic.

### Task 2 – RASE Analysis

Identify all:

* Requirements
* Applicability conditions
* Selections
* Exceptions

Provide justification for each classification.

### Task 3 – Ambiguity Analysis

For every potentially ambiguous statement:

* Explain alternative interpretations.
* Explain which interpretation is preferred.
* Explain why.

### Task 4 – Human-Level Evaluation

Before assigning a RASE category, ask:

* Is this a legal requirement?
* Is this only a measurement method?
* Is this merely supporting information?

If it is only supporting measurement methodology, explain why it should or should not be represented as a RASE element.

### Task 5 – Final Output

Produce:

1. A detailed legal interpretation.
2. A RASE summary table.
3. A machine-readable IF/THEN representation.
4. A hierarchical structure showing relationships between general rules, special rules, and exceptions.
5. A short explanation of how your result compares to a human expert's interpretation.

Regulation:

§47 Aufenthaltsräume

Aufenthaltsräume müssen eine lichte Raumhöhe von mindestens 2,40 m haben.

Aufenthaltsräume im Dachraum müssen eine lichte Raumhöhe von mindestens 2,20 m über mindestens der Hälfte ihrer Netto-Raumfläche haben; Raumteile mit einer lichten Raumhöhe bis zu 1,50 m bleiben außer Betracht.

Die Sätze 1 und 2 gelten nicht für Aufenthaltsräume in Wohngebäuden der Gebäudeklassen 1 und 2.

Additional Expert Guidance:

* The attic-room provision should be analysed as a special rule that overrides the general requirement for standard habitable rooms.
* The statement regarding areas with a clear height of 1.50 m or less is controversial and should be carefully evaluated as either measurement methodology or an independent RASE element.
* Human reviewers concluded that semantic interpretation is more important than keyword-based classification.
